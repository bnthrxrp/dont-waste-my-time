## Gap fills (validator research)

- **EV1** Scale words differ by Portuguese variant. CLDR pt (Brazil): 10^9 = 'bilhão' (short 'bi'), 10^12 = 'trilhão' (short 'tri'). CLDR pt-PT: 10^9 = 'mil milhões' (short 'mM'), 10^12 = 'bilião/biliões' (short 'Bi'), 10^6 short 'M'. Microsoft's pt-PT style guide nevertheless tells localizers to render English 16,000,000,000 as '16 biliões', while its pt-BR guide says '16 bilhões', so the pt-PT guidance conflicts with CLDR. Runtime Intl (CLDR 47): pt-BR 1.5e9 = '1,5 bi' or '1,5 bilhão'; pt-PT = '1,5 mil milhões'. Currency symbols: BRL is 'R$' in pt, pt-PT and en; USD is 'US$' in pt and pt-PT but '$' in en, so a bare '$' in English text about Brazil is ambiguous. Source: CLDR JSON numbers and currencies for pt, pt-PT, en; Microsoft Localization Style Guides, Portuguese (Portugal) and Portuguese (Brazil), section 'Numbers', CLDR main branch, runtime-checked on CLDR 47 (Node 22 / ICU 77.1); Microsoft pt-PT guide PDF created 2024-04-10, modified 2024-09-04, <https://raw.githubusercontent.com/unicode-org/cldr-json/main/cldr-json/cldr-numbers-full/main/pt-PT/numbers.json>. Why critical: Resolves the dossier's open 'bilião' question. A pt-PT source or reviewer can turn 'billion' into a 1000x error in either direction, which is the highest-severity error class for executive numbers.
- **EV2** In Brazilian tax and commercial usage, 'faturamento' is tied to sales of goods and services documented by invoice and is treated as equivalent to 'receita bruta'. Lei 9.718/1998 equated faturamento with receita bruta. The STF (RE 390.840) rejected stretching it to all income. Lei 12.973/2014 unified receita bruta as sales of goods, services, third-party operations and revenues of the principal activity. The article says the concepts moved between legislation and court rulings and gives no single settled definition. Separately, a search result (not read in full) says Lei 6.404/76 art. 187 opens the income statement with receita bruta, less deductions, giving receita líquida. Source: Faturamento, receita bruta e receita (Migalhas de Peso; Fernandes and Moreira, Fernandes, Figueiredo, Françoso e Petros Advogados), 2018-12-12, updated 2019-10-04, <https://www.migalhas.com.br/dePeso/16,MI292639,81042-Faturamento+receita+bruta+e+receita>. Why critical: Corrects the dossier's glossary rule. The practical trap is gross (faturamento / receita bruta) versus net (receita líquida), not invoicing versus revenue. Mistranslating it changes the headline top-line figure of nearly every Brazilian management deck.
- **EV3** CPC 26 (R1) item 51(d)-(e) requires entities to show the presentation currency and the level of rounding used in the statements. Item 53 says presenting information in thousands or millions of currency units is acceptable provided the rounding level is disclosed and material information is not omitted. Item 52 says this is normally satisfied by page, statement, note and column headings. Source: CPC 26 (R1) Apresentação das Demonstrações Contábeis, items 51-53 (CVM consolidated Rev. 14), unknown (consolidated Rev. 14), <https://conteudo.cvm.gov.br/cvm_institucional/export/sites/cvm/menu/regulados/normascontabeis/cpc/CPC_26_R1_rev_14.pdf>. Why critical: Grounds a rule the dossier missed: the unit and scale live in table or column headers ('R$ mil', 'Em milhões de R$'). Dropping or mistranslating one header silently shifts every figure in the table by 10^3 or 10^6.
- **EV4** Microsoft's pt-BR localization style guide instructs the use of non-breaking spaces between a unit of measure or currency and its number (section 4.1.8). It says to spell out one to ten, to render high round numbers as 'mil', 'quatro milhões' and '16 bilhões' (section 4.1.9), and to avoid word-to-word translation because it makes the tone stiff and unnatural (section 2.1.4). CLDR also uses U+00A0 in the pt currency and compact patterns. Source: Portuguese (Brazil) Localization Style Guide, Microsoft, PDF created 2024-02-09, modified 2024-09-04, <https://download.microsoft.com/download/8/e/3/8e349e32-9eb9-4b63-9909-7586b94a24dd/por-bra-StyleGuide.pdf>. Why critical: Gives a concrete, checkable typography rule (NBSP) and a named authority against calques. Product-localization context, applied here by analogy to business documents.

## Exclusions

- **Full MQM numeric scoring (severity multipliers, error-type weights, per-1,000-word normalization, calibrated quality score):** Built for sampling 500-20,000 word projects and comparing vendors. For executive documents a Critical-zero gate plus Major/Minor counts is enough, and the multipliers are explicitly non-normative.
- **WMT24 Error Span Annotation protocol and 0-100 segment scores:** A research protocol for ranking systems. The Minor/Major/Critical tagging in the review pass already captures what is useful.
- **BLEU, COMET, chrF and GEMBA-MQM style automated scoring or LLM-judge pipelines:** WMT24 shows metric leaders can differ from human leaders, and the judges are black-box. Not usable as sign-off, and they add complexity.
- **Round-trip translation as a score, and LMArena/LiT benchmark results (E9):** Model-level benchmark with no document-level validation; the paper itself warns that a correct round trip does not imply correct intermediate translations. Keep only as optional triage.
- **ISO 17100 certification, translator qualification and project-management requirements, and quoting ISO in deliverables:** Only the workflow shape is useful. The standard excludes raw MT plus post-editing, and the normative text was not read.
- **Transcreation as a separate service tier:** Marketing-oriented, sources disagree on boundaries, and the skill covers business materials. Keep only the faithful/adaptation labeling.
- **MQM Neutral severity level and the Untranslated subtype:** Neutral is preference-only and adds nothing to a fix-or-not decision. The Untranslated subtype was not found on the typology page.
- **IBM expansion buckets by source length and the 13%-30% Portuguese figures as hard numbers:** Old, generic and inconsistent. Use the 30% headroom plus a render check.
- **Exhaustive pt-BR versus pt-PT vocabulary lists and a 'passivo corrente is European' claim:** Unverified (vendor blog), large, and low value for executive materials. Keep only the numeric/format differences and the bilião warning.
- **Microsoft 'voice' guidance (você by default, informal tone):** Product UI voice, not executive register. Only the NBSP and anti-calque points are borrowed.

## Unresolved contradictions

- pt-PT scale words: CLDR pt-PT gives 10^9 = 'mil milhões' and 10^12 = 'bilião', but Microsoft's pt-PT style guide renders English 16,000,000,000 as '16 biliões'. Unresolved, so the rule is to verify magnitude and never carry 'bilião' into pt-BR output.
- 'Faturamento': CPC 47 uses it for the invoicing event (bill-and-hold), while Brazilian tax law and business usage equate it with receita bruta (gross revenue). Resolved only as context-dependent, with gross revenue as the management-deck default. The business-usage evidence is one law-firm article plus search snippets.
- Portuguese text expansion is unresolved: about 13% (pt-BR, no method), 15-25%, 20-30% and about 30%, with short labels expanding far more. The rule uses headroom plus a render test.
- ISO 17100 current status (Amendment 1, confirmed 2020, ISO/AWI 17100 in 2025) cannot be resolved from accessible sources. It does not affect the rules.
- Whether back-translation detects numeric or omission errors in business text is unresolved. The only primary paper (E9) is model-level and warns about intermediate errors, so it stays triage-only.
- Automatic and LLM judges (GEMBA-MQM, E8) versus human-as-final-judge (E6) are not reconciled for Portuguese business text. The skill defers to human sign-off and labels its own revision pass as a model check.
- Whether 'resultado' alone is statutory profit or loss or operating result cannot be settled from CPC 26, because item 8 allows 'lucro líquido' as a label for the period result. This is left to line-level context and a query.
