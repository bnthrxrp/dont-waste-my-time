# Validation report B-dataviz (Layer 2, Sonnet)

**Dossier quality:** 3.5/5

- B1 overstates Heer & Bostock: the paper says angle was NOT shown to be worse than length, contrary to theory. The implication 'length next, then angle and area' is not supported.
- B4 and the dual-axis rule rest on Datawrapper's characterization of Isenberg 2011. That study tested Dual-Scale (focus plus context) charts, not two-measure dual y-axes. The dossier also missed Datawrapper's July 2026 reversal on dual-axis charts.
- The implementation rationale for pies says the paper 'reports added error' for exploded, varying-radius, icon and nested pies. Skau & Kosara only reason about these, and say exploded pies may be fine for some tasks. 'Never use' is a conservative choice, not a finding.
- Numeric thresholds (3 pie slices, 8 colors, 'close values' for overlay) are not experimentally derived. Only the eight-color limit has a source (Wilke, practitioner).
- Several statistics were not checked in the dossier but are verifiable. Examples are the Bateman N=20, Ajani N=24 undergraduates, and the Kim and Kong sample sizes. All are small or crowdsourced.
- Coverage gaps: no evidence on distributions, scatter plots, stacked bars, sorting, legends vs direct labels, tables vs charts, or sequential and diverging palettes. B17 was marked not accessed although the slides were downloadable.
- The 'implementation_recommendations' mix evidence-backed rules and the agent's own heuristics without labeling them. Strengths, numbers and caveats are otherwise honestly reported, and the accessed flags were accurate.

## Gap fills (validator research)

- **BV1** Witt (2019, Meta-Psychology): across five experiments (N per experiment 9-20, bar and line graphs of simulated standardized effects), a y-axis range set just beyond the data made effects look big, and the full 0-100 range made them look small. Sensitivity was best and bias smallest at about 1-2 SDs (1.5 recommended). Axis range is a design choice with consequences in both directions. The finding is for standardized scientific effect sizes; it gives no formula for business metrics, and it conflicts with a strict zero rule when bars show small standardized effects. Source: Graph Construction: An Empirical Investigation on Setting the Range of the Y-Axis, 2019 (Meta-Psychology vol. 3, MP.2018.895), <https://open.lnu.se/index.php/metapsychology/article/view/895>. Why critical: The dossier treats axis honesty as 'zero vs not zero'. This is the only controlled evidence that too-wide ranges also mislead, which matters for line charts and for KPIs where a zero baseline hides a material change.
- **BV2** Kale, Kay & Hullman (MTurk, eight uncertainty designs): adding means to uncertainty visualizations biases magnitude and decision judgments consistently with discounting uncertainty. Users treat visual distance between point estimates as a proxy for effect size, and the designs that gave the least biased effect-size estimates were not the ones that supported the best decisions. The authors note the common practice of omitting uncertainty leads to 'incredible certitude'. Source: Visual Reasoning Strategies for Effect Size Judgments and Decisions, 2020 (arXiv v3, 12 Sep 2020; IEEE TVCG), <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.14516v3.pdf>. Why critical: Executive materials are dominated by point estimates and forecasts. The dossier's uncertainty evidence was only about how error bars are drawn; this adds evidence that a visible point estimate makes readers discount the range, which supports showing the range beside the number.
- **BV3** WCAG SC 1.4.3 (AA): normal text needs at least 4.5:1 contrast; large text (18pt, or 14pt bold) needs 3:1; values are not rounded (4.499:1 fails); incidental text, logotypes and decoration are exempt. This is the text counterpart to the dossier's 3:1 rule for chart marks. Source: Understanding SC 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum), Updated 06 September 2026, <https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html>. Why critical: The dossier covered mark contrast (1.4.11) but not the contrast of chart labels, axis text and annotations. Light-grey axis and footnote text is the most common contrast failure in decks and dashboards, and direct labels are exempt from 1.4.11 only if their text passes 1.4.3.
- **BV4** Datawrapper's July 23, 2026 post says it changed its mind: dual-axis charts launched for Business plan users. The reasons are a well-designed Financial Times dual-axis chart and a practitioner argument that dual axes preserve absolute levels, which indexed charts lose. They suit readers who compare when each metric peaked. The post cites no formal studies. The 2018 article stays, with its concerns kept for general audiences. Source: why i changed my mind on dual axis charts (Datawrapper blog), 2026-07-23, <https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/why-i-changed-my-mind-on-dual-axis-charts>. Why critical: B4 relied on this vendor as the main evidence for a hard ban. The vendor has since softened its position, and no controlled study of two-measure dual y-axes exists in the dossier or in my checks, so the rule must be a conservative default with named exceptions.

| Start the value axis of every bar or column chart at zero. Never truncate it and never use an axis break. Use identical axis ranges for charts the reader is meant to compare, across panels and across slides. | B2, B3, BV1 | moderate | Line, dot and range charts of time series or differences; not standardized-effect-size science graphs (Witt's SD range is not transferable to business KPIs) | reference:dataviz |

Do not encode the primary quantity by area (bubbles, treemap size), volume/3D, or angle (pie, donut, radial bars) when a common-baseline bar can carry it. Do not claim a donut is more or less accurate than a pie. | B5, B1 | moderate | Part-to-whole exhibits | reference:dataviz |

## Exclusions

- **Munzner's expressiveness/effectiveness vocabulary and the full channel-ranking taxonomy:** Adds jargon without changing any output; the useful content is already encoded in the bars-on-a-common-baseline rule. The ranking figure is an image in the slides, so the exact middle order was not text-verified.
- **Full IBCS SUCCESS framework, hatching and notation as a default:** Specifics are unverified (standard paywalled, vendor pages disagree) and most executive audiences do not use IBCS. Offered only as optional-module:ibcs-notation when requested or already in use.
- **Gradient plots and violin plots for executive uncertainty displays:** Tested in Correll & Gleicher only on MTurk inferential tasks; unfamiliar to directors and a documented cultural cost. Prefer labeled ranges or dot-and-interval.
- **Memorability-driven choice of unusual chart types (Borkin et al.):** Memorability was measured on single images, not decision accuracy; unusual forms cost decodability. Only a single accent color for the key message is kept.
- **Embellishment or imagery as a recall aid (Bateman et al.):** 20 participants (10 per recall condition), one designer's style, and the authors themselves are cautious. It also conflicts with the declutter evidence, so no rule either way is justified.
- **Statistics seen only in search snippets: Lo et al. 2022 (axis choice in 28.6% of 1,143 charts), Pandey et al. percentages, Eells 1926 self-reports:** Not read at the primary source in this validation; not suitable for rules or citations.
- **Witt's SD-based axis range (1.5 SD) as a business rule:** Derived from standardized effect sizes in small experiments (N 9-20); business KPIs have no equivalent standard. Only the principle that too-wide and too-narrow ranges both bias perception is kept.
- **Donut hole size and donut-versus-pie debates:** Study 2 shows no meaningful accuracy difference; nothing actionable beyond 'do not claim donuts are better or worse'.
- **Claims that decluttering improves trustworthiness or comprehension:** Ajani et al. found no significant trustworthiness effect and no comprehension benefit from decluttering alone; focus effects were measured on recall after 10-second views.
- **A blanket '8% of men are colorblind' figure:** Applies to European-ancestry men; Birch gives 4-6.5% for Chinese and Japanese men and 0.4% (Wilke: 0.5%) for women. Use a range or omit.
- **Heer & Bostock Experiments 2 and 3 (gridline alpha, chart size and gridline spacing):** Not read by the dossier author; no rule can be drawn.

## Unresolved contradictions

- Zero baseline for bars (Correll et al., Datawrapper) vs Witt 2019: for standardized science effects, a zero-based or full-range axis made effects look too small and sensitivity was best at about 1-2 SD. Witt's samples were tiny (9-20 per experiment). The practical resolution (bars stay at zero, small deltas get an explicit number or a dot/line chart) is a design judgment, not settled evidence. Whether explicit axis-break cues are acceptable remains unresolved, since Correll found cues did not de-bias viewers.
- Dual-axis charts: Datawrapper's 2018 ban cites a study that did not test two-measure dual axes, the vendor reversed its product stance in July 2026 while keeping its concerns, and no controlled study of dual y-axes was found. The conservative default is practitioner opinion, not a finding.
- Neutral, non-evaluative titles (Kong et al.) vs focus headlines and action titles (Ajani et al.): reconciled here by quantified factual titles, but Ajani's participants also said focused graphs 'seemed to have an agenda', so how far to push a recommendation in the headline is untested.
- Embellishment (Bateman: no accuracy loss, better 2-3 week recall, n=20) vs declutter (Ajani: professionalism gains, no comprehension gain, prior literature mixed and some studies preferring cluttered designs): no resolution; the skill takes no stance beyond keeping imagery out of labels and axes.
- Mechanism for pies: Heer & Bostock found angle not worse than length, Skau & Kosara found angle the least important cue (readers use arc length and area), Munzner-style rankings place angle below length. The practical conclusion (label values, prefer bars) is stable; the mechanism is not.
- Overlay vs small multiples: Javed (separate panels win for wide visual spans, 2/4/8 series, 16 participants) vs Ondov (overlay strong for pairwise comparison). Task-dependent; no series-count threshold exists in the evidence.
- IBCS green/red variance convention vs the color-alone and CVD rules: resolvable with redundant cues such as sign, arrow or text, but the primary IBCS 2.0 / ISO 24896 text was not read to confirm how it handles color-vision deficiency.

## Needs verification

- Cleveland & McGill (1984) original ranking, especially angle vs length. Heer & Bostock contradict the theoretical order, and Munzner's ranking figure is an image that was not text-verified.
- Franconeri et al. (2021) full text (not open access): chart-type-specific recommendations, pie-chart guidance, and statements on legends vs direct labels are unread.
- Pandey et al. (CHI 2015) primary results on truncated and inverted axes; only secondary reports and Witt's mention were seen.
- IBCS 2.0 / ISO 24896 primary text (ISO store, CHF 135): scenario encodings, zero-baseline rule, variance color rules, and accessibility handling. The Lapajne AFP 2015 article was not re-read.
- No evidence gathered for: stacked bars and segment comparison, bar sorting, legends vs direct labels, tables vs charts, distributions and scatter plots, sequential/diverging palettes, small-multiples thresholds, and number of charts per slide.
- Numeric thresholds (2-3 pie slices, about 4 overlaid series, about 12 labeled bars, 8 colors) are heuristics. Only the 8-color limit has a practitioner source (Wilke); Munzner's slides say 6-12 bins.
