# Validation report D-dashboards (Layer 2, Sonnet)

**Dossier quality:** 4/5

- Honest about access limits and confidence; most numeric claims check out against the sources.
- D17 headline ('up to 75% hallucinated') is stated without the caveats that it is a peak figure and that 'hallucination' means non-matching insights; the paper's own error taxonomy shows fabrication is a small share.
- D7 paraphrases are loose: the 6-12 graph is two panels (trailing six weeks, trailing year by month) and is one of three visualisation types, not a mandated 'last six periods vs prior year' layout; publication dates were available (2 Jul 2024; correction 24 Jan 2025) but marked unknown.
- D4 implication adds 'pie charts' that neither Wikipedia nor the eye-tracking study mention.
- D6 uses the later 'learning and growth' label for a 1992 article that used 'innovation and learning'; primary text still unread.
- D13 AICD content was readable from the PDF (the dossier called it unreadable) and is richer than reported: risk, background and recommendation also have page caps; the AICD summary does not contain the recommendation, unlike the synthesised rule.
- D16 underplays the topic-familiarity effect (6% vs 28-29% within one model), which makes the cross-study 'no improvement' comparison with D15 uninformative.
- No 2020-2026 dashboard-comprehension evidence was filled in; the 2024 Information & Management experiment (524 participants) was found but its abstract could not be read.
- Several rules (polarity, guardrail pairing, RAG tolerances, flag caps) are inferences with no direct source and must stay labelled as such.

## Gap fills (validator research)

- **DV1** WCAG Success Criterion 1.4.1 Use of Color (Level A): colour must not be the only visual means of conveying information, indicating an action, prompting a response or distinguishing an element. The Understanding document says the intent is to make colour-conveyed information available to users with colour deficiencies by also providing it through another visual means (text, number, symbol, pattern). This is a normative accessibility standard and gives firm backing to the 'never colour-only status' rule, replacing the dossier's reliance on a Wikipedia sentence about single-hue bands. Source: Understanding Success Criterion 1.4.1: Use of Color (WCAG 2.2), WCAG 2.2 W3C Recommendation, 2023 (the 1.4.1 criterion dates from WCAG 2.0); page read 2026-10-30, <https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html>. Why critical: RAG/traffic-light status is the most common director-dashboard encoding and the dossier's colour rule rested on weak secondary evidence; this gives a primary, testable standard.
- **DV2** SEC Regulation S-K Item 303 (17 CFR 229.303): where revenue changes materially, MD&A must describe the extent to which the change is attributable to price changes, to changes in volume/amount of goods or services sold, or to new products or services (303(b)(2)(iii)); must describe known trends or uncertainties with material impact (303(b)(2)(ii)); and 'must not merely repeat numerical data contained in the financial statements' (Instruction 2 to paragraph (b)). This is the primary-source anchor for variance commentary that decomposes drivers and explains rather than restates; the dossier's only support was an unverified secondary summary and vendor guides. Source: 17 CFR 229.303 - (Item 303) Management's discussion and analysis (Cornell LII mirror of eCFR), Current CFR text as read 2026-10-30 (rule amended 2020; exact amendment date not verified), <https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/229.303>. Why critical: Variance commentary is a core director deliverable and the dossier had no non-vendor source for the price/volume/mix-and-no-restating convention.
- **DV3** Stephen Few's definition of a dashboard: 'a visual display of the most important information needed to achieve one or more objectives', consolidated and arranged on a single screen so it can be monitored at a glance. The fetched reprint is dated 17 March 2004 (search results give 20 March 2004 for the Intelligent Enterprise original). This is the primary source for the single-screen/at-a-glance convention that D2 calls 'screenfit'. Search results also report his 2007 follow-up separating dashboards (monitoring) from 'faceted analytical displays' (analysis), and note the single-screen rule is contested for mobile and wall displays; I did not read those. Source: Dashboard Confusion (Stephen Few, Intelligent Enterprise / InformationWeek reprint), 2004-03-17 (reprint date on fetched page; original cited as 2004-03-20 in search results), <https://www.informationweek.com/data-management/dashboard-confusion>. Why critical: The dossier's screenfit rule relied on a catalogue entry and an unread Few PDF; the primary definition clarifies that single-screen applies to monitoring dashboards, which resolves the tension with multi-page genres in Sarikaya et al. and Bach et al.
- **DV4** FinanceBench abstract page (arXiv 2311.11944, submitted 20 Nov 2023): 10,231 questions about publicly traded companies; authors evaluated 16 model configurations on a 150-case sample; GPT-4-Turbo with a retrieval system incorrectly answered or refused to answer 81% of questions. Models are 2023 vintage and the task is question answering over filings, not report drafting, so the rate is not transferable to current models; it does show that LLM handling of figures in financial documents is unreliable enough to require checking. Fills the dossier's open question about numeric reliability, with those limits. Source: FinanceBench: financial question answering benchmark (arXiv 2311.11944), 2023-11-20, <https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11944>. Why critical: The dossier had citation-fabrication evidence but nothing on errors in numbers drawn from financial documents, which is the main risk in director dashboards and KPI reports.

## Exclusions

- **Balanced Scorecard four-perspective frame as a required structure:** Primary 1992 text unread and evidence on effectiveness is conceptual; only the lagging-vs-leading idea is useful and it is covered by the input/output rule. A mandatory four-quadrant layout adds decoration.
- **Copying Amazon WBR scale or mechanics (400-500 metrics, 60-90 minute meeting, 6-12 graph as a mandated layout, deck order):** Specific to one company's operating cadence; deck order is contradicted within the source; the article says the format is not a hard constraint.
- **Page-count and reading-speed targets for board packs (30 pages/hour, 226-page average, 120 pages in four hours):** Vendor surveys (n=50; mixed years); mean 226 pages vs median about 200 and 40% under 100 pages; the 30 pages/hour figure is an advisory benchmark. Use purpose-first pruning instead.
- **84% of directors want more focused packs:** Appears only in a search-result title; not on the fetched page.
- **'Up to 75% of LLM summaries is hallucinated' as a quoted rate:** Peak on one benchmark, defined as non-matching insights rather than false claims; fabrication was only 0-9% of the authors' error categories.
- **Claims that bullet graphs improve accuracy or comprehension, or a pie-chart ban attributed to this stream:** The only experiment measures time to first fixation in an industrial setting; pie charts are not covered by any source read. Leave pie-chart guidance to the dataviz stream.
- **Mandatory use of Bach et al.'s eight pattern groups or genre names as vocabulary in outputs:** No evidence that using the patterns improves decisions; naming patterns to executives adds jargon. Use them as an internal checklist only.
- **Goodhart taxonomy labels and 'watermelon reporting' as terms in executive documents:** Useful for Claude's reasoning, but jargon for director readers; the rules already carry the behaviour (guardrail metric, written status rules, automatic reds).
- **Numeric RAG tolerances (for example +/-5% amber, +/-10% red) or fixed caps on number of KPIs/flags:** No source provides them; tolerances must come from the owning team. Common figures such as 7+/-2 or a '5-second test' have no support in this stream.
- **Deloitte refund amounts (A$97,000 vs final instalment):** Conflicting and unverified; irrelevant to rules. Use the case only for the verification lesson.
- **Nature 2026 hallucinated-citation percentages, ICAEW AI-errors guidance, Kumar et al. 2021 BSC review:** Found only in reposts, navigation-only fetch, or snippets; not read.
- **Emotive and other colour patterns from the catalogue:** No evidence or director-use case; adds complexity.

## Unresolved contradictions

- Single screen vs multi-page: Few's definition (and 'screenfit') requires one screen for dashboards, while Sarikaya et al. and Bach et al. treat narrative/analytical dashboards and 'multiple page' as legitimate and the rule is contested for mobile. Working resolution by scope (monitoring = one screen; analytical/narrative = page one stands alone), but no empirical test exists.
- Amazon WBR deck order: the Commoncog body says the deck ends with financials; the 24 Jan 2025 correction says WBRs open with financials. Unresolved without the primary source (Bryar and Carr). Do not state a deck order.
- Executive-summary content: AICD limits the summary to 4-5 lines of purpose and ask with the recommendation in a separate section; Board Intelligence says key points go up front with no length. Whether the recommendation belongs in the summary depends on document type; no evidence compares the two.
- Board pack length: Board Intelligence reports a 226-page average, an earlier Cambridge study a median near 200 pages, and 40% of packs under 100 pages; an aggregator's '24% over 200 pages' is unverified. Cannot be reconciled; do not use numeric length targets.
- Citation fabrication trend: 18% (GPT-4, 2023) vs 19.9% (GPT-4o, 2025) is not comparable because topic familiarity alone moved GPT-4o from about 6% to 29%; no evidence either way on whether newer models are safer.
- Deloitte refund amount: AP says the final instalment of an A$440,000 contract is to be repaid, amount unpublished; CFO Dive's A$97,000 could not be found or verified.
- Alert-fatigue transfer: the healthcare study finds repeats and volume of alerts reduce acceptance but 'no general effect of workload'; whether this holds for executive KPI flags is untested, and conventional advice to cap flags has no numeric evidence.
