## Dossier quality

**Score: 4/5** (accuracy 4.5, coverage 3).

What is good: every quote and number I could re-read matched (HBR 2011 checklist, Klein procedure, Nutt 356 decisions and Table 4 values, Eisenhardt abstract, Bain RAPID/2010/2013 pages, Spetzler six elements, Minto site and profile). Limitations were labelled honestly, and the revision correctly refused to propagate unreadable McKinsey claims.

Issues:
- **Source concentration.** A1-A5 are five "findings" from one HBR article (A5 is a citation inside it), so the source count overstates breadth. Real independent sources: HBR 2011, Klein, Nutt, Eisenhardt, Bain (3 pages), Spetzler (interview), Minto (site and profile), Schoemaker.
- **A1 interpretation contradicted by its own source.** The dossier proposes the checklist as an author's pre-send self-review. HBR 2011 says "because you can't recognize your own biases, the individuals using this quality screen should be completely independent from the teams making the recommendations" (V1). Self-check is a weaker substitute, not the intended use. (The earlier validator flagged the same.)
- **A5 / AS6 over-cautious.** Both said the Lovallo and Sibony figures could not be checked and told Claude not to cite 6.9 pp or "six times". The article text is readable on a McKinsey-owned domain (V2), so those figures can now be quoted, with qualifiers (see R17). HBR's "up to seven percentage points" and the article's 6.9 pp are the same comparison, which resolves the dossier's flat-vs-up-to disagreement.
- **A6 presents Klein's 30% as a study result.** Klein does state it (verified), but the Mitchell, Russo and Pennington abstract I retrieved does not support it (see Claim checks).
- **A14 over-claims.** The McKinsey page it cites (read via V3) does not use "logic tree" or "strong hypotheses early".
- **A15 not reproducible.** The 68-student, 50% widening and ten-step claims are not on the Sloan HTML page, which is truncated; I could not locate them elsewhere.
- **A11 vs AS1 vs Bain history.** The two dossiers carry different Agree-role readings ("cannot veto" vs "required sign-off"). Bain's own articles from 2006, 2011 and 2026 differ, and neither dossier captures that (see Unresolved contradictions).
- **A12 / AS7 attribution.** Minto credits Aristotle with the underlying idea and describes her own contribution as the abbreviation and its application to groups of ideas; "Minto developed MECE" is overstated.
- **Weak sources still in play.** A9 (abstract-level), A12 and A16 (Wikipedia), A13 (vendor page, hypothetical case) should carry little weight.
- **Dossier rule risk.** "Include a how-this-was-produced note" invites an LLM to fabricate dissent or consultation; it needs an honesty constraint (R7).
- **Coverage gaps.** No evidence on executive comprehension of answer-first structure (none found) and no outcome evidence for MECE or issue trees. GF1 and GF2 partially fill the headline and hypothesis-testing gaps by analogy only.

## Gap fills

Three, with sources I read.

- **GF1. Claim-style slide headlines (the dossier's stated open question).** Garner and Alley (2013), *International Journal of Engineering Education* 29(6), 1564-1579, full text extracted. Randomised comparison of 110 undergraduate engineering students (67 men, 43 women) hearing the same narrated MRI lecture with either common-practice slides (phrase headline plus bullets, violating six multimedia principles) or assertion-evidence slides (sentence headline, visual evidence). Essay comprehension and misconceptions favoured assertion-evidence (e.g. t(93.43)=3.67, p<0.001, d=0.81 on one essay measure); delayed one-week sentence-completion recall d=0.89; perceived mental effort lower. Limits: one topic, one lecture, students, six principles changed together so the sentence headline is not isolated, and the participants rated the common-practice text amount as more appropriate. Transfer to executive decks is by analogy. Why critical: the only controlled evidence on action titles.
- **GF2. Evidence for hypothesis-testing over intuition.** Camuffo, Cordova, Gambardella and Spina, *Management Science* (online 2019; the 2020 volume), full text extracted, abstract confirmed. RCT of 116 early-stage Italian startups over about a year; both groups got 10 sessions of general training, the treated group was also taught to build predictive frameworks and test hypotheses "very much as scientists do". Treated entrepreneurs "perform better, are more likely to pivot to a different idea, and are not more likely to drop out". I did not read effect-size tables. Setting is startups, not consulting deliverables. Why critical: the dossier's hypothesis-first default had only practice-based support; the McKinsey primaries (V3, V8) describe the practice but give no outcome data.
- **GF3. The full 12-question HBR checklist, read from the text (V1)**, so rules can be mapped to checkable items:
  1 motivated or self-interest errors in the team; 2 team "fallen in love" with the proposal; 3 dissent within the team and whether explored; 4 diagnosis driven by salient analogies; 5 credible alternatives considered; 6 "if you had to make this decision again in a year, what information would you want"; 7 do you know where the numbers came from; 8 halo effect; 9 overly attached to past decisions; 10 base case overly optimistic; 11 worst case bad enough; 12 team overly cautious. Rules R5, R6, R9, R10 and R11 are the items a model author can check on its own output; items 1-4 and 8-9 need a human reviewer who knows the proposers.

Placement values: core, reference:analysis, reference:writing, optional, exclude. Count thresholds and wording quotas below are skill design choices, not findings, unless stated.

## Exclusions

- **Printing the 12-question HBR checklist or Spetzler's six elements in deliverables.** They are review aids. Use only as an internal check (R18) and as a prompt for a human reviewer.
- **Presenting any of these as established fact:** "reducing bias raises returns up to 7 points" or "process matters six times more than analysis" without the R17 qualifiers; Klein's 30%; Schoemaker's 50% or 68-student result; Nutt's "half fail" without its definition. Bain's "95% confidence" correlation must never be cited (no method given, AS3).
- **Naming McKinsey's seven steps as McKinsey-sourced method.** The primary page read does not enumerate them (V8). "Logic trees", "day-one answer" and "simple data before algorithms" are the only McKinsey wording verified.
- **Attributing "governing thought", "answer first", "SCQA" or a "right number of supporting points" to Minto.** None appears on her concept page (AS4) and no primary source gives a number (AS "disagreements").
- **"Minto invented MECE".** Her own account credits Aristotle and claims only the abbreviation and its application.
- **Mandatory or visible MECE.** Critiques are conceptual and no outcome study exists (A12, AS7).
- **RAPID letters or "Agree has/has no veto" as fact.** Bain's 2006, 2011 and 2026 articles differ.
- **"Top consultancies use MECE issue trees as their primary tool".** Uncited (revision's own flag).
- **Hypothesis-first as proven for consulting deliverables.** GF2 is startups; keep it as practitioner default with R8's disconfirmation step.
- **Scenario planning as a default step and the ten-step process.** The article body is unverified (A15); optional only.
- **80/20 as a law or default split (A16), Eisenhardt's "use more information" as a general rule (abstract only, eight firms), Dean & Sharfman (A9), Stratrix's pricing example as a real case (A13).**
- **A full "how this was produced" narrative.** Replaced by the R7 Basis note.
- **Premortem or full alternatives treatment for routine decisions (A4).**
- **Fixed counts as if sourced** (3-5 or 3-7 premortem causes, branch counts, tree depth).

## Unresolved contradictions

1. **RAPID Agree, three Bain statements.** 2006 (Rogers and Blenko): a few Agree holders may carry a veto, and a vetoing party must offer an alternative or escalate to the D. 2011 (Blenko, Mankins, Rogers): Agree must sign off before the recommendation can move forward, Input cannot veto, and no veto wording appears. 2026 (Cochemé and Mottura): the A role has no veto and unresolved disagreement goes to the D. The revision dossier's "sign-off, not veto" and A11's "cannot veto" are both defensible readings of different articles. Unresolved; R12 forces the document to state blocking power.
