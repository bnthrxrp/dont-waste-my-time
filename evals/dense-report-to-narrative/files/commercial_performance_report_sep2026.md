# Northwind Consumer Health — Commercial Performance Report
## Period: January–September 2026 (YTD) | Prepared by Commercial Excellence | Version 3 (final)

### 1. Introduction
In today's rapidly evolving and highly competitive consumer health landscape, it is more important than ever to keep a close eye on our commercial performance. This report aims to provide a comprehensive and holistic overview of all relevant aspects of our commercial results for the year to date, leveraging the full breadth of data available to the Commercial Excellence team. As in previous editions, the report covers market context, regional performance, price and volume dynamics, channel inventory, promotions, portfolio, customer service and outlook. We would like to thank all teams for their continued dedication and hard work, which has been instrumental in navigating a challenging environment.

### 2. Market context
The US OTC market grew 3.9% in value and 0.6% in units over the period (scanner data, all outlets combined). Inflation in the category remained elevated, with average shelf prices up 3.3%. Private label continued to gain share, reaching 24.8% of category value (+1.1 points year over year). Cold and flu incidence was in line with the five-year average. Digestive health was the weakest segment (-2.4% value). Pain relief grew 5.1%. E-commerce now represents 17.2% of category value versus 15.0% a year ago. Our overall value share was 11.4%, down 0.3 points.

### 4. Price, volume and mix
Year-over-year net sales growth of +4.0% breaks down as follows: price +6.8%, volume -3.1%, mix +0.5% (multiplicative decomposition). The budget assumed price +4.5% and volume +1.5%. The list price increase of February 2026 was fully implemented across all customers by April. Volume declines were concentrated in Digestia and Brevex value packs, where private label price gaps widened to 38% and 31% respectively.

### 5. Sell-in and sell-out
Sell-in (our shipments to customers, net sales value) grew +4.0% year over year. Sell-out (consumer offtake at retail, scanner data, value) grew +0.7% over the same period. Trade inventory at our top 20 customers rose from 5.2 weeks of supply in December 2025 to 6.8 weeks in September 2026, against a customer-agreed range of 4 to 6 weeks. Average weekly sell-out at these customers was $7.9M in Q3. Several customers have signaled that they intend to bring inventory back within the agreed range before year-end.

### 6. Trade promotions
Trade spend reached $58.4M YTD versus $49.5M in the prior year (+18.0%), equal to 14.1% of gross sales (prior year: 12.4%). Of the 11 national promotions run this year, 3 delivered an ROI below 1.0 (incremental gross profit divided by promotion cost). The average ROI across all national promotions was 0.82 versus 1.07 in the prior year. The two largest promotions by spend (Brevex BOGO in January and Relivia 30% off in June) had ROIs of 0.61 and 0.74.

### 8. Customer service
On-time-in-full (OTIF) averaged 91.2% YTD against a 95.0% target (prior year: 94.1%). The main driver was a packaging supplier issue at the Columbus plant between April and July. Customer compliance fines totaled $1.9M YTD (prior year: $0.6M).

### 9. Outlook
The current full-year forecast is $548M of net sales versus a budget of $556M (98.6%), assuming that Q4 sell-in continues at the run-rate of Q3. The forecast does not include any impact from customer inventory reductions.

### 10. Conclusion
Overall, the year to date shows a solid performance in a challenging environment, with growth versus prior year, a strong Relivia season and good regional momentum in the Northeast and Southeast. The team remains committed to delivering the full-year plan.
